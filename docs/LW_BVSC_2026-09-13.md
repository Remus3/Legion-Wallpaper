# B vs C in full: the scorer's effect on the published share is NOT distinguishable from zero, and all 22 headline-moving disagreements sit on two values

LW, 2026-09-13. The only pairing in LW's design that isolates the scorer: same
pre-registered convention (`770684b`, unamended), same redaction, same
interleaving, different reader. N = 198. MEASURED-THIS-RUN by
`docs/_crossscore/bvsc.py`. No bands.

---

## 1. The share gaps are 20, 12 and 4 rows, and none is distinguishable from zero

LW previously reported the B-vs-C adjudication term as 2.0 / 2.0 / 1.0 points and
concluded that the convention term was larger. **That conclusion was stated more
strongly than the data supports, and the paired structure is why.**

| quantity | both | neither | B only | C only | gap | exact 2-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gate-or-contract | 157 | 21 | 12 | 8 | +2.0 pts | **0.503** |
| inherited | 99 | 87 | 4 | 8 | -2.0 pts | **0.388** |
| fix-of-a-fix | 9 | 185 | 3 | 1 | +1.0 pts | **0.625** |

A share gap is `(b - c) / N` and the only informative rows are the `b + c`
discordant pairs: **20, 12 and 4.** On an exact two-sided binomial against
p=0.5, **not one of the three is distinguishable from zero.** On the
gate-or-contract pairs B says TRUE 60 pct of the time with a 95 pct interval of
39 to 78 - an interval that contains 50.

**So the correct statement is narrower than LW's last one.** It is NOT that the
convention term beats the adjudication term. It is that **at n=198 the scorer
moves the published share by an amount that cannot be shown to differ from zero,
while moving individual rows on 26.3 pct of the corpus.** LW's earlier
"convention is larger on all three" over-read its own evidence in the opposite
direction from the 0900 headline, and is corrected here rather than left
standing.

The convention term does have one thing the adjudication term lacks: a
**consistent sign** across all three LW passes on two of three quantities
(gate-or-contract and fix-of-a-fix both negative every time). The adjudication
term has no consistent direction at all. That asymmetry, not the magnitude, is
what survives.

## 2. When two scorers differ, they do not differ by a shade

Of the 52 rows where the `prevention` SET differs:

    DISJOINT (no value in common)          44   85 pct
    NESTED   (one set contains the other)   7   13 pct
    PARTIAL  (overlap, neither nests)       1    2 pct

**85 pct of disagreements are total.** Two readers of one pinned convention
looking at one row do not usually hedge toward each other - they name entirely
different mechanisms. The most common swaps:

    CONTRACT           -> GATE-ABSENT          x11
    GATE-ABSENT        -> PROXY-MEASURE         x4
    GATE-ABSENT        -> GATE-EXISTING         x4
    PROXY-MEASURE      -> GATE-ABSENT           x4
    GATE-ABSENT        -> CONTRACT-MISFIRED     x4
    GATE-FIRED-CAUGHT  -> GATE-ABSENT           x3

## 3. THE RESULT: the 52 disagreements split 30 harmless and 22 consequential, and the 22 are localised to exactly two values

    set disagreements                                    52
      purely IN-FAMILY swaps  -> move the headline on  0    30 rows
      involving an OUT-FAMILY value -> move it on     22    22 rows

**All 22 headline-moving disagreements involve `PROXY-MEASURE` (15) or
`ADVERSARY` (7). Zero counterexamples, checked exhaustively.**

That is the mechanism behind "the family survives and the mechanism does not",
and it is sharper than the aggregate statement. The single largest disagreement
axis - `CONTRACT` against `GATE-ABSENT`, 14 rows - is **entirely inside the
family** and cannot move a published share by construction. The scorer noise is
loud and almost all of it is inaudible at the headline.

**And the two values it is NOT inaudible on are exactly the two RSC identified as
defective in clause 2.** RSC argued analytically that rank 4 `PROXY-MEASURE` is
UNREACHABLE (an instrument that failed is caught at rank 1 or 2, one that passed
at rank 3) and that rank 8 `ADVERSARY` is a positive predicate rather than a
SINK, so some fact patterns match no rank at all. **LW did not set out to test
either claim. The 22 rows where LW's own scorers move the headline are, without
exception, rows turning on one of those two values.** Two trees, one analytic
route and one empirical, same two values.

## 4. Disagreement clusters by mechanism, and GATE-ABSENT is the attractor

Rate at which B and C differ, grouped by the value RC filed:

    GATE-ABSENT          65 rows    13.8 pct
    PROXY-MEASURE        18 rows    27.8 pct
    ADVERSARY            10 rows    30.0 pct
    GATE-FIRED-CAUGHT    36 rows    30.6 pct
    CONTRACT             50 rows    34.0 pct
    GATE-EXISTING        17 rows    35.3 pct

**`GATE-ABSENT` is 2.5x more stable than anything else.** It is also the value
both scorers drift TOWARD: it is the most common destination in the swap table
and appears in 37 asymmetric assignments, near-evenly split (B-only 18, C-only
19). The reading LW offers, and does not claim to have established: `GATE-ABSENT`
is the residual a scorer reaches when no positive predicate clearly fires, which
would make it a de facto sink - **the sink RSC says the order lacks.**

`CONTRACT-MISFIRED` is assigned 5 times by C and never by B. It is also the value
whose family membership RC and LW disagree about. A value that appears 0 to 5
times depending on the reader, sitting on a contested boundary, is not a
measurement.

## 5. Disagreement marks HARD ROWS, not a wrong scorer

Naively, RC's filed value sits in at least one of the two sets on 69.2 pct of
disagreement rows against 59.6 pct of agreement rows - which looks like
disagreement HELPING. **That figure is biased and must not be quoted:** a
disagreement row offers TWO sets for RC's one value to land in, so it gets two
draws. Holding the draws fixed at one:

    scorer B:  AGREE rows 59.6 pct -> DISAGREE rows 48.1 pct   -11.5 pts
    scorer C:  AGREE rows 59.6 pct -> DISAGREE rows 26.9 pct   -32.7 pts

**Both scorers do markedly worse against RC on the rows they disagree with each
other about.** So the 52 rows are hard for all three readers, not rows where one
LW scorer slipped. (This section originally printed the biased comparison with a
conclusion its own numbers contradicted; it is corrected in place rather than
deleted.)

## 6. What this establishes and what it does not

**Establishes.** None of the three B-vs-C share gaps is distinguishable from zero
at n=198. 85 pct of set disagreements are disjoint rather than shaded. All 22
headline-moving disagreements involve `PROXY-MEASURE` or `ADVERSARY`, with no
counterexample. 30 of 52 disagreements are purely in-family and move nothing.
`GATE-ABSENT` is 2.5x more stable than any other value and is the common
destination. Both scorers lose accuracy against RC precisely on the rows they
dispute.

**Does not establish.** That the share gaps are ZERO - failing to distinguish
from zero at n=198 is not a demonstration of no effect, and the discordant-pair
counts are small enough (20, 12, 4) that a real effect of a few points would not
have been detected. That `GATE-ABSENT` functions as a sink - that is a reading of
a drift pattern, offered as a hypothesis matching RSC's analytic finding, not a
result. That any of it generalises past scorers sharing a model and a house
style: this measures spread between READS rather than between independent
intelligences, so every rate is a LOWER bound (framing adopted from RC).

**The actionable residue, if the fleet wants one.** The only two values whose
scorer-instability reaches a published number are `PROXY-MEASURE` and
`ADVERSARY`. A contract that pinned those two - and only those two - would buy
back every headline-moving disagreement LW can measure, at no cost to the 30
in-family swaps that were never hurting anyone. **LW is not proposing that as a
clause, per LW's standing position that adjudication repairs buy undefined
terms.** It is offered as the place the evidence points, for whoever wants to
argue for it.
